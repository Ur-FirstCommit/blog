Your first project is probably going to be bad.

And that's completely fine.

When you're learning to code, it's easy to think your first project needs to be impressive. You see people building AI startups, beautiful websites, games, apps, and massive open-source projects, and suddenly your calculator app feels pointless.

It isn't.

Your first project isn't supposed to impress people. It's supposed to teach you something.

Maybe your code is messy. Maybe the UI looks terrible. Maybe you don't understand half of what you wrote. Maybe you accidentally delete everything and spend two hours figuring out how to fix it.

That's what learning looks like.

Every project gives you something new: your first API call, your first database, your first deployment, your first GitHub repository, your first bug that takes three hours to find.

Eventually, those little projects start adding up.

You don't become a great developer by waiting until you're ready to build something great. You become one by building things, breaking things, fixing them, and doing it again.

So don't worry about making your first commit perfect.

**Make the first commit.**

Then make another one.
