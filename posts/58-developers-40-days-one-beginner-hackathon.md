A behind-the-scenes look at how **Beginner's Paradise** went from an idea to **58 participants and growing** in _roughly_ 2 weeks.

## The Idea

I wanted to create a hackathon for beginners.

Most hackathons can feel intimidating when you're just starting out. There are often experienced developers, complex projects, and expectations that you already know how to build something impressive.

But everyone has a first project. Everyone has a first commit.

The goal of Beginner's Paradise was simple: create a space where new developers could learn, experiment, and build something they are proud of — regardless of their experience level.

Instead of focusing only on the final product, the hackathon focuses on the journey: learning new skills, making progress, and showing what you built.

## The Launch

After planning the idea, setting up the rules, and creating the event listing, Beginner's Paradise officially launched.

At the start, there was no guarantee that people would join. Creating an event is easy; getting people excited enough to participate is the difficult part.

The first few days were about spreading the word, answering questions, improving the event page, and building a community around the idea.

Slowly, registrations started coming in.

## The First 10

Reaching the first 10 participants was a milestone.

Those first participants proved that the idea was something people were interested in. They weren't just signing up for a hackathon — they were joining a community built around learning and creating.

Every registration after that made the event feel more real.

## Then Suddenly: 58

In less than two weeks, Beginner's Paradise reached **58 participants**.

What started as a simple idea had become a growing community of developers ready to build, learn, and share their projects.

And the exciting part?

There are still **40 days left** before submissions close.

The community is still growing, and more beginners are joining every day.

## What Happens Next

The next phase is all about helping participants succeed.

Over the coming weeks, we will be supporting participants through announcements, resources, community discussions, and updates.

The goal is not just to run a hackathon — it is to make sure everyone who joins learns something new and finishes with something they can be proud of.

## The Learning

Organizing Beginner's Paradise has already taught me a lot.

Creating a hackathon is much more than just making an event page. There are participants to support, judges to organize, sponsors to contact, communities to build, and countless small details behind the scenes.

Every step has been a learning experience.

## The Goal

The goal of Beginner's Paradise has always been simple:

Help someone make their first commit.

Whether someone builds their first website, their first app, or their first software project, the important part is starting.

58 developers have already started their journey.

Now let's see what they build.
